ICECCME 2026 · Motivation and evaluation questions

Why two evaluation questions?

Primary: alignment with Japanese human ratings

How close are Japanese model scores to Japanese human ratings?

Reference: Japanese human VAS means

Secondary: translated-condition drift

How much do English and Chinese model scores change from Japanese?

Reference: the same model's Japanese scores

Less change across languages does not prove closer agreement with humans.

Stable scores can still disagree with readers.

Our primary question compares Japanese model scores with Japanese human ratings.

Our secondary question compares translated conditions with the same model’s Japanese scores. These are different references.

ICECCME 2026 · Experimental design

Three works, four emotions, twelve fixed cells

Japanese human reference

  • 91 student responses
  • 86–91 usable ratings per cell
  • Interest, surprise, sadness, anger
  • Visual Analogue Scale (VAS): 0–100

Neutral-reader model task

  • 6 models × 3 languages × 3 works × 10 repetitions
  • One task; temperature 0.4
  • 540 valid runs; 2,160 scores
  • 90 rows retried; no imputation

Works: T1 · The Pocket Watch; T2 · The Money and the Pistol; T3 · The Tattered Ostrich.

Collection: OpenRouter · 17–19 April 2026 (JST). The six models are listed in Table I.

We use three works and four emotions: interest, surprise, sadness, and anger. Scores range from zero to one hundred.

We collected ninety-one student responses, with eighty-six to ninety-one usable ratings per cell.

Six models scored three languages and three works, with ten repetitions. We used one neutral-reader task at temperature zero point four.

The April seventeen to nineteen, twenty twenty-six collection produced five hundred forty valid runs and two thousand one hundred sixty scores.

Ninety rows needed retries. No scores were imputed.

ICECCME 2026 · Metrics, uncertainty and sensitivity

Two distances, with uncertainty checks

Two references

  • JA MAE: Japanese model vs human means
  • Drift: EN/ZH vs the same model's JA means
  • Absolute differences over 12 cells
  • Average drift = (EN drift + ZH drift) / 2

Two checks

  • 10,000 cluster-bootstrap replicates
  • Respondents + runs; 95% intervals
  • Omit each of 3 stories / 4 emotions
  • 7 deterministic composition checks

Intervals are conditional on the fixed 12 cells, not new literary works.

We average ten repetitions per cell. Both distances average absolute differences over twelve cells.

MAE means mean absolute error. It compares Japanese model and human means. Drift uses the same model’s Japanese means as reference.

Average drift is the mean of English and Chinese drift. Lower values mean closer agreement or less change, respectively.

We used ten thousand cluster-bootstrap replicates and ninety-five percent percentile intervals.

Respondents and runs were resampled independently. Each run keeps four emotions together.

Each replicate uses the same human profile for all models. Intervals are conditional on twelve fixed cells, not new works.

We also omit each story and emotion separately to check composition sensitivity.

ICECCME 2026 · Cell errors and composition sensitivity

Error direction depends on the story

Signed error = model mean − Japanese human VAS mean · Red: higher · Blue: lower

Now look at the right panel. Signed error is model mean minus Japanese human mean.

T3 sadness is the largest shared error. All six models overestimate it. Its mean absolute error across models is 48.525.

All six underestimate T2 sadness and overestimate T2 surprise. Their mean absolute errors are 39.083 and 33.860, respectively.

All six also underestimate T1 sadness. The direction depends on the story.

All seven omissions changed the full order: three stories and four emotions. These checks show sensitivity, not a causal explanation.

ICECCME 2026 · Limitations and conclusions

Stage-one screening needs broader validation

Limited coverage

  • 3 works; 4 fixed emotions
  • One student-reader population
  • 6 selected models; one neutral task
  • No matched EN/ZH human reference

Limited interpretation

  • Text + instructions change language
  • Translations not independently validated
  • Translation provenance incomplete
  • Provider routes and aliases unpinned

Report uncertainty, cell errors and composition sensitivity; then validate more broadly.

Our pilot covers three works, four emotions, one student population, six models, and one neutral prompt.

We lack matched English and Chinese human ratings. Fixed translations were team-checked, not independently validated.

The translation system, version, and date were not recorded. We cannot separate language, translation, and cultural effects.

Provider routes and model aliases were unpinned. Exact API replay may change.

Conditional intervals do not generalize to new works, readers, prompts, or languages.

Report uncertainty, cell errors, and composition sensitivity alongside rankings.

This is stage-one screening, not deployment approval. Broader validation needs more works, readers, prompts, and matched multilingual human references.
