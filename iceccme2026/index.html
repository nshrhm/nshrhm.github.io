ICECCME 2026 · Study overview

How close are AI ratings to readers?

Read three literary works → rate interest, surprise, sadness and anger.

1 · Human alignment: primary

Japanese texts → human ratings

Japanese texts → model scores

Compare models with Japanese human means.

2 · Translated conditions: secondary

EN/ZH texts + instructions → model scores

Compare with the same model in Japanese.

No EN/ZH human reference.

Human agreement and translated-condition stability answer different questions.

When people read a story, they may feel interest, surprise, sadness, or anger. We ask how closely AI scores match those reader ratings.

The left path is our main experiment. Students read Japanese texts and rated their emotions. Models scored the same works in Japanese.

We then compared the model averages with the human averages. The human ratings are our reference.

The right path asks a different question. We changed the text and instructions to English or Chinese and compared each model with itself.

There are no English or Chinese human ratings here. Stable scores can still disagree with readers.

ICECCME 2026 · Metrics, uncertainty and sensitivity

Two distances, with uncertainty checks

Two references

  • JA MAE: Japanese model vs human means
  • Drift: EN/ZH vs the same model's JA means
  • Absolute differences over 12 cells
  • Average drift = (EN drift + ZH drift) / 2

Two checks

  • 10,000 cluster-bootstrap replicates
  • Respondents + runs; 95% intervals
  • Omit each of 3 stories / 4 emotions
  • 7 deterministic composition checks

Intervals are conditional on the fixed 12 cells, not new literary works.

We average ten repetitions per cell. Both distances average absolute differences over twelve cells.

MAE means mean absolute error. It compares Japanese model and human means. Drift uses the same model’s Japanese means as reference.

Average drift is the mean of English and Chinese drift. Lower values mean closer agreement or less change, respectively.

We used ten thousand cluster-bootstrap replicates and ninety-five percent percentile intervals.

Respondents and runs were resampled independently. Each run keeps four emotions together.

Each replicate uses the same human profile for all models. Intervals are conditional on twelve fixed cells, not new works.

We also omit each story and emotion separately to check composition sensitivity.

ICECCME 2026 · Conclusions

A practical starting point for evaluation

  1. Measure both axes: human alignment and translated-condition stability.
  2. Look beyond averages: uncertainty, cell errors and composition sensitivity.
  3. Use stage-one screening before broader human validation.

Next: more works, broader reader populations, more prompts and matched multilingual human references.

This study gives us a practical starting point. Measure human alignment and translated-condition stability separately.

Read average scores together with uncertainty, cell errors, and composition sensitivity. Our results do not establish a definitive model ranking.

Use this compact benchmark for stage-one screening. It does not approve a model for deployment.

The next step is broader validation with more works, readers, prompts, and matched multilingual human references.

English prompt: role, definitions and rules

Neutral-reader role

“a neutral reader who calmly reads the whole work and reports post-reading emotions without exaggeration”

Scoring rules

“rate the text as a whole after reading it”

“keep each reason brief and grounded in the text”

Emotion definitions

  • interest: How funny, entertaining, or intellectually enjoyable the text feels
  • surprise: How unexpected, novel, or revelatory the text feels
  • sadness: How sad, melancholic, or poignant the text feels
  • anger: How much anger, indignation, or displeasure the text evokes

These are selected excerpts from the saved English request. The full request also contains the work, scale anchors, and output instructions.

The definitions specify the four dimensions. They are the experimental wording, not a general theory of emotion.

One neutral-reader task was used. We did not compare different reader personas in this study.

ICECCME 2026 · Q&A reference · Limitations

Where the evidence stops

Limited coverage

  • 3 works; 4 fixed emotions
  • One student population; no demographic stratification
  • 6 selected models; one neutral task
  • No matched EN/ZH human reference

Limited interpretation

  • Text + instructions change language
  • Translations not independently validated
  • Translation provenance incomplete
  • Provider routes and aliases unpinned

Report uncertainty, cell errors and composition sensitivity; then validate more broadly.

Our pilot covers three works, four emotions, one student population, six models, and one neutral prompt. Readers were not stratified by demographics.

We lack matched English and Chinese human ratings. Fixed translations were team-checked, not independently validated.

The translation system, version, and date were not recorded. We cannot separate language, translation, and cultural effects.

Provider routes and model aliases were unpinned. Exact API replay may change.

Conditional intervals do not generalize to new works, emotions, readers, prompts, providers, languages, or models.

Report uncertainty, cell errors, and composition sensitivity alongside rankings.

This is stage-one screening, not deployment approval. Broader validation needs more works, readers, prompts, and matched multilingual human references.
