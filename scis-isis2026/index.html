Cross every persona with every temperature

Two separate controls

Source: paper, Method and Experiments; fully crossed design figure.

To address this problem, we vary persona and temperature independently. Persona is a reading stance, and temperature is a sampling control.

The rows show four personas. The columns show four temperature settings.

We observe every combination, rather than one temperature for each persona.

For each model and text, we repeat each combination five times. The task and response format stay fixed.

Only the reading stance changes in the prompt. Temperature is set separately through the API.

The persona labels are categories, not points on a psychological scale.

This design lets us describe persona, temperature, and their interaction separately within each model, text, and emotion.

Scores and entropy answer different questions

Mean score

How high is the reported emotion intensity?

Response scores → Cell mean

Normalized fuzzy entropy H*

How do scores overlap the Low, Medium and High membership regions?

Each score → H* → Cell mean

Use one fixed membership family for comparisons.

H* describes membership overlap, not human emotional uncertainty.

Source: Method; Membership Specification and Entropy.

The mean score tells us the reported intensity of an emotion.

Fuzzy entropy asks a different question. We map each score to Low, Medium, and High membership values.

The resulting entropy describes membership overlap under this fixed specification.

We calculate entropy for each response first, then average within the cell. We do not transform the cell mean.

A higher entropy value does not mean that a human reader feels more uncertain.

The two outcomes come from the same responses, but they describe different features. We therefore examine their interaction patterns separately.

What these results do—and do not—show

Supported here

  • Separate description of two controls
  • Persona-dominated score shares
  • Larger interaction shares for H*

Still unverified

  • Agreement with human readers
  • Generality beyond this fixed panel
  • Provider-independent causal effects
  • External validity of membership choices

Five repeats and a sensitivity check support a bounded diagnostic.

Source: Discussion; Limitations and Future Work.

These results support a design diagnostic within our selected panel.

They do not establish agreement with human readers. We did not perform human-reference validation in this study.

Five repeats describe some repeat-level stability, but do not establish that five are sufficient.

Temperature values may have different behavioral meanings across providers.

Entropy also depends on the membership specification. Our sensitivity check is not external validation.

Broader texts, deeper replication, and human-reference studies are needed before extending these conclusions.

Separate the controls, examine both outcomes

  1. Cross the controls independently.
    Observe every persona–temperature combination.
  2. Read scores and entropy separately.
    The same responses can show different separability patterns.

Design the comparison before interpreting the differences.

Source: Discussion and Conclusion.

Let me conclude with the main lesson: vary the two controls independently, and examine both outcomes.

That makes their separate contributions and interaction observable.

In this study, mean scores were largely persona-driven and nearly additive. Fuzzy entropy retained more interaction.

So a stable-looking score pattern does not guarantee the same pattern for a derived descriptor.

We recommend specifying the design and the membership family clearly, then interpreting each outcome within those conditions.

How the decomposition is defined

For one model, text and emotion: ȳ is a repeat mean in the 4 × 4 table.

SSpersona=4∑p(y‾p⋅−y‾⋅⋅)2,SS_{\mathrm{persona}} = 4\sum_p(\bar{y}_{p\cdot}-\bar{y}_{\cdot\cdot})^2,

SStemp=4∑τ(y‾⋅τ−y‾⋅⋅)2,SS_{\mathrm{temp}} = 4\sum_\tau(\bar{y}_{\cdot\tau}-\bar{y}_{\cdot\cdot})^2,

SSpersona×temp=∑p,τ(y‾pτ−y‾p⋅−y‾⋅τ+y‾⋅⋅)2.SS_{\mathrm{persona}\times\mathrm{temp}} = \sum_{p,\tau} (\bar{y}_{p\tau}-\bar{y}_{p\cdot}-\bar{y}_{\cdot\tau} +\bar{y}_{\cdot\cdot})^2.

B=SSpersona×tempSSpersona+SStemp+SSpersona×temp,B = \frac{SS_{\mathrm{persona}\times\mathrm{temp}}} {SS_{\mathrm{persona}} + SS_{\mathrm{temp}} + SS_{\mathrm{persona}\times\mathrm{temp}}},

Separability share = 1 − B. Repeat variation is assessed by bootstrap,
not added as a fourth sum-of-squares component.

B is normalized within each cell-mean table.

Source: Method, equations (1)–(4), converted to native MathML.

These are the manuscript equations. The factors of four follow the balanced four-by-four design.

We use the same decomposition for the mean score and the mean of per-response normalized entropy.

The denominator includes persona, temperature, and interaction only. Repeat-level variation is handled through bootstrap intervals.

The reported panel summaries average these within-unit shares. They are not a pooled global decomposition.
